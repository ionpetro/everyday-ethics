# Everyday Ethics: commitments, influence and action

Authored November 10, 2026. This is an educational course design and content review, not an empirical finding that the app improves real-world ethical behavior.

## Scope and lineage

Units u07–u12 use the existing Agora semantic contract and inherited family engine: a taught principle, an understandable concept, a conditional scenario, a different retrieval form, misconception repair, and a cold transfer case. The output is literal course data, not one prewritten multiple-choice question per lesson. There are 24 lessons and 144 semantic items in these six units, including 24 principles with two distinct inline checks each and 48 original scenarios. All scenario people and events are invented.

## Sources and limits

- **E1:** [Markkula Center, A Framework for Ethical Decision Making](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making/), revised November 5, 2021; accessed November 10, 2026. Grounds the course's attention to affected people, relevant facts, multiple ethical perspectives, alternatives and review. It is normative guidance, not experimental evidence that a particular dialogue increases trust or guarantees an outcome.
- **E2:** [Kwan, McLean and Raicu, Introduction to the Framework](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/introduction-to-a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making/), November 8, 2021; accessed November 10, 2026. Explicitly limits the framework: users still exercise judgment, lenses may conflict, the process may need revisiting, and the framework is not a complete survey of ethical traditions.
- **E3:** [ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics), adopted June 22, 2018; official [PDF](https://www.acm.org/binaries/content/assets/about/acm-code-of-ethics-booklet.pdf) and publisher-hosted search excerpts consulted November 10, 2026. Direct page and PDF opens returned HTTP 403; indexed primary-source excerpts supplied the relevant sections, so this was not a successful full-document retrieval. Relevant sections include 1.2 harm, 1.3 honesty and interests, 1.6 privacy, 1.7 confidentiality and 2.5 evaluation. This is a professional computing code. Everyday analogies are the course author's practical synthesis, not a claim that the code legally governs every friend or volunteer.

No text of a code is presented as jurisdiction-independent law. No professional eligibility, medical guidance, diagnosis, emergency protocol or promise of legal compliance is taught. No studies or numerical effectiveness claims are invented. The course does not reproduce source exercises or claim the sources endorsed its exact answer keys. Scenario scoring depends on expressly stated facts and purposes; open moral conflicts remain a matter for reflective labs.

## Teaching and safety decisions

Every lesson has a held-back transfer tied to its earlier rule. A misconception repair provides a substantive boundary case; for example, actual deadlines are not all manipulation, public availability does not settle every reuse, private reporting can be courageous, and repayment does not force renewed access. Ethical choices are never speed-scored. Eleven explicitly enabled neutral definitions may appear in quick recall; they ask for concept recognition, not a verdict on a person.

Fieldwork uses fictional, low-stakes examples. It does not require participants to disclose private experiences, confront a powerful person, publish allegations, or take over another person's complaint. When authority or safety appears in a scenario, the relevant route and constraints are stated. Participation, feedback and boundaries are treated as meaningful rather than inferred from silence.

- Independent reviewer (`dilemma_labs`) read all u07–u10 teaching checks, options, sorting and matching. Corrected u09-l4-practice: counting likes is not amplification, so the item now explicitly reposts an accusation because it has many likes. Improved u10-l4's irrelevant font distractor to a plausible confusion between registrations and attendance.
- Independent reviewer found two unstated facts in u12-l1. Added the explicit fact that both venue options fit the budget, and made the neighborhood committee's evaluation, choice and review date explicit before asking which record describes that decision.
- These structural and editorial checks do not establish learning efficacy or settle philosophical disagreements. More varied user testing would be needed to assess difficulty and transfer to everyday decisions.

## Cross-review of the foundations units
